Health Risks

Vaping is becoming more and more popular by the year. Social media and the internet are not slowing it down either. Social media is why so many teenagers actually do it. Vaping looks like it’s harmless because of the colors and flavors, but it is actually harmful. Vapes contain something called nicotine — an extremely addictive substance that can change how your body and mind function. This can lead to problems with attention, memory, and learning, which are all things a teenager needs for high school and college.

When you inhale vapor from a vape, you're not just breathing in flavored air. You’re actually inhaling chemicals like formaldehyde, acetaldehyde, and acrolein. These can damage the lungs badly. Over time, this can lead to chronic coughs, breathing problems, and even conditions like popcorn lung. Although people say that vaping is a healthy alternative to smoking, studies say that vaping increases the chance of heart disease. Vaping is also more addictive than smoking because a vape has more nicotine than a casual cigarette. A single vape has more nicotine in it than several packs of cigarettes.

How Vaping Affects the Brain

Vaping impacts how a teen’s brain develops. Nicotine changes how the cells in your brain communicate, which affects memory, learning, and focus. Since teen brains are still growing, vaping can change the brain for addiction, making it harder to quit and increasing the chance of using other drugs in life.

What’s in the Vapor?

  • Nicotine (highly addictive)
  • Formaldehyde - This is used to preserve dead bodies
  • Heavy metals
  • Diacetyl - Causes serious lung diseases
  • These substances go straight into your lungs with every puff.

Short-term vs Long-term Health Effects

Short Term:

  • Coughing or Wheezing
  • Shortness Of Breath
  • Increased Heart Rate
  • Trouble Sleeping Or Focusing

Long Term:

  • Lung Damage and popcorn lung
  • Increased risk of Heart Disease
  • Higher Chances of Developing Asthma or Chronic Bronchitis
  • Long Lasting Addiction
